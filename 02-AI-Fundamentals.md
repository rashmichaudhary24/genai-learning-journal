# AI Fundamentals

## Resource(s) Studied

* IBM Think – *What is Artificial Intelligence?*

## What I Learned

The most important mental model I took away from this article is:

> Artificial Intelligence ⊃ Machine Learning ⊃ Deep Learning

Before reading this article, I tended to think of AI, machine learning, and deep learning as overlapping buzzwords. The article clarified that they represent nested concepts:

* Artificial Intelligence is the broad field of building systems that can perform tasks requiring human intelligence.
* Machine Learning is a subset of AI in which systems learn patterns from data rather than being explicitly programmed.
* Deep Learning is a subset of machine learning that uses multilayer neural networks and powers many modern AI applications.

The article also introduced Generative AI as a further application of deep learning and foundation models.

## What Surprised Me

The most striking idea for me was that AI transformation is not simply about adding AI tools to existing processes.

As Jonathan Adashek observed:

> "This is not just about putting AI on top of old ways of working. This is about putting AI at the centre of the organisation and building around that."

This changed how I think about AI adoption. AI may represent an organizational transformation challenge as much as a technology challenge.

## Enterprise Implications

As someone coming from learning, capability building, and organizational transformation, I found several implications particularly relevant:

* AI adoption is fundamentally an organizational change initiative.
* Responsible AI and governance are strategic concerns, not merely technical ones.
* Leaders need sufficient AI literacy to make informed decisions.
* Organizations will need to redesign workflows, roles, and learning ecosystems around AI capabilities.

## Personal Reflection

One idea that stayed with me after studying AI fundamentals was Jonathan Adashek's observation that AI should not simply be added to existing ways of working.

This led me to formulate a metaphor that resonated with me:

> **AI isn't a garnish. It's an ingredient.**

A garnish decorates an existing dish. An ingredient changes the nature of the dish itself.

I believe organizations that treat AI as an add-on may achieve incremental improvements, while organizations that redesign their operating models around AI may achieve transformational outcomes.
